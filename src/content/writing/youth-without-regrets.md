Regret rarely comes from the things we tried that didn't work out. More often, it comes from the versions of ourselves we never gave a real chance.

In your twenties, it is easy to get obsessed with making the "optimal" move—waiting until a plan feels bulletproof, waiting until you feel qualified, or waiting for someone to hand you certainty. But youth is the one season where the cost of experimenting is lowest and the upside of curiosity is highest.

## Bias toward lived experience

Looking back at the moments that shaped me most, almost none of them looked tidy on paper:

- **Saying yes to steep learning curves:** stepping into roles and projects slightly above my comfort zone and figuring them out along the way.
- **Traveling and paying attention:** packing a camera, taking buses across borders, and letting unfamiliar places reset how I think.
- **Building things for the sake of seeing them work:** turning late-night ideas into real pages, campaigns, and communities instead of leaving them in a notes app.

Not every bet pays off right away. Some projects stall, some paths close, and some lessons sting. Still, every honest attempt leaves you sharper than standing still ever could.

## No half-hearted seasons

Living without regrets doesn't mean living recklessly. It means giving full effort to whatever season you are in—working with intention when it is time to build, being fully present with the people you care about, and not letting fear of looking inexperienced keep you on the sidelines.

> A youth without regrets isn't one without mistakes—it is one where you actually showed up for the life you wanted to build.
