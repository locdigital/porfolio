People love to misunderstand the phrase *fake it till you make it*. They hear "fake" and assume it means pretending to know things you don't, bluffing your way through rooms, or selling a story with nothing behind it.

That version never lasts. Reality catches up fast—in client calls, in campaign numbers, in code that either ships or breaks.

The version that actually works is much simpler: stepping into the posture of the work before you feel fully qualified for it, and then working hard enough to close the gap.

## Posture comes before comfort

Almost nobody feels ready for the next level when it first arrives. Your first real client, your first big budget, your first project where the outcome sits entirely on your shoulders—all of them arrive slightly before your confidence does.

If you wait until you feel 100% certain, you stay at the same level forever. In practice, healthy "faking it" looks like:

- **Borrowing composure when you don't have certainty:** staying calm in the room, asking clear questions, and refusing to let nervousness dictate your decisions.
- **Accepting the responsibility first:** saying yes to a challenge slightly above your current skill ceiling, then treating the deadline as a forcing function to learn fast.
- **Holding yourself to a higher standard early:** presenting your work cleanly, communicating clearly, and keeping promises like someone who has done it ten times before.

## The gap only closes through reps

Confidence without competence is just noise. The only reason the phrase works at all is the second half: *till you make it*.

Every time you step into a room that feels a little too big, you owe yourself the quiet hours afterward—reading, testing, fixing what broke, and turning borrowed confidence into real skill. Do that enough times and something subtle happens: you stop feeling like you are acting the part, because the evidence has finally caught up with the standard you set.

## When it stops being fake

One day you look up and realize you are no longer guessing your way through the fundamentals. You know what questions to ask, what signals to ignore, and how to recover when a plan goes sideways.

You didn't trick anyone. You simply refused to let early self-doubt keep you out of the arena long enough to earn your place in it.

> Fake it till you make it isn't about fooling other people—it's about acting with the standards of who you are becoming until the work makes it true.
